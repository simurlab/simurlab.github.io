Collaborate with us

We work with companies, federations, mutual insurers and clinical teams that need human movement measured where it happens: at a workplace, on a track or at home. This page says what we can do, what backs it and how a collaboration is set up.

Write to juan@uniovi.es

What we can do for a partner

Each line of work below rests on published results or on completed contracts.

A measurement system for your movement variable

You name the variable that matters to you, a joint angle at a workstation or a contact time in a sprint, and the people who will use the system. We develop the sensor set-up, the algorithms and the software, test them in the laboratory and then in the field.

Backed by
Upper-limb joint angles at real workplaces, with sensors placed by prevention technicians, for FREMAP (CMBBE 2016); sprint running analysis with sensors on the shoes, for the Real Federación Española de Atletismo (2025).

Independent evaluation of a wearable device

We compare inertial sensors and motion capture systems with a reference: a robotic test bench for the sensor and its algorithm, and an optical laboratory for measurements on people. The result is an error figure with the conditions in which it was obtained.

Backed by
Commercial and in-house inertial sensors compared on a robotic bench (IEEE TIM 2026); evaluation of an optical motion capture system with an industrial robot (Sensors 2021).

MovLab and the robotic test bench

Sensor nodes for demanding conditions

When commercial sensors do not reach the range or the bandwidth of the movement, impacts and fast sport gestures for instance, we design the wearable node and characterise it: noise, bias stability, scale factor and axis misalignment.

Backed by
Design and characterisation of a wearable inertial node (Sensors 2024) and of an extended-bandwidth version for rapid events (Sensors 2025).

Analysis of movement and activity data

If you already record data with wearable sensors, we extract gait variables and recognise activities from them, and we tell you how the result depends on where the sensor is worn and on which signals are used.

Backed by
Activity recognition from thigh and wrist sensors in a multi-centre study (Sensors 2026); ground contact time from sensors on the arm and the back (Sensors 2023); training assessment in kayak paddlers with a smartphone (J. Sports Eng. Technol. 2023).

How a collaboration works

  1. 1. The measurement problem

    A first conversation about what has to be measured, on whom, where, and who will place and operate the sensors. It commits neither side.

  2. 2. A written proposal

    Scope, tasks, deliverables, schedule and budget. Research contracts are signed with the University of Oviedo and managed by its foundation (Fundación Universidad de Oviedo).

  3. 3. Laboratory first

    The method is checked against a reference in the MovLab laboratory before it leaves the building.

  4. 4. Field tests with your people

    We test in your setting, with your staff or athletes, find the limits of the method and adjust it.

  5. 5. Delivery

    Software, equipment or a report, as agreed, with the conditions in which the results hold.

Terms we agree at the start

  • Ownership and confidentiality. Who owns the results and what can be published are written into the contract. In past contracts the partner has owned the results, and publications have required its consent.
  • Data on people. Recordings of workers, patients or athletes follow data protection rules and, where it applies, the approval of a research ethics committee.
  • Public funding. A collaboration can also take the form of a joint application to a regional, national or European call, with the partner as participant or as interested party.
  • Stated limits. We report the error of a measurement together with the conditions in which it was verified, and we say so when a result has not been validated.

Who we have worked with

  1. Mutual insurer

    FREMAP. Three successive contracts between 2010 and 2015 on joint angle measurement at the workplace; the first system was used by its prevention technicians.

  2. Sports federation

    Real Federación Española de Atletismo. Capture and analysis of sprint running with inertial sensors (2025).

  3. Companies

    Human Analytics. Processing methods for the assessment of sports practice with inertial sensors (2022). Loutkar Robotics, a developer of rehabilitation devices, sponsors work in the group.

  4. Health research

    Instituto de Salud Carlos III and the Principality of Asturias have funded our work with clinical teams on wearable technology in primary care and on ageing.
